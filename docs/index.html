Can March Signals Prioritize Content for April Review?

Ashritha Chary | FlyRank ML Internship | 29 September 2026

Abstract

This study asks whether March measurements can rank content items at higher risk of an April impressions drop for limited editorial review. It queries the FlyRank internship warehouse's daily performance fact table and content dimension, yielding 143,206 eligible March–April content/client pairs across 45 clients from a release containing about 79 million daily records. Regularized logistic regression and histogram gradient boosting were compared with a fixed Week-4 rule using five client-held-out folds, while random-row splits were retained only as a weaker diagnostic. Across client-held-out folds, logistic regression achieved mean precision@50 of 0.688 versus 0.392 for the rule and a test base rate of 0.517; because model selection used those same folds, this result is exploratory rather than an untouched test. The result supports further evaluation of human-review prioritization, but it does not establish refresh uplift, predict search rankings, or guarantee performance on future months.

01 · DECISION

Introduction / Problem statement

An editor has more pages to review than time to review them. The practical question is whether observable search and content signals can help order that review without pretending that a score knows which edit will work.

This study asks: using measurements available by 31 March 2026, can a model rank eligible content items that will show a large observed impressions drop in April more effectively than a transparent rule? The intended output is a review order for a human editor, not an automated refresh decision.

The unit is one pseudonymized content item within a client. A false positive spends review time on an item that does not cross the proxy threshold; a false negative leaves a declining item lower in the review order. Precision@50 measures the share of the first 50 ranked items that meet the proxy label. The test base rate is shown beside it so ranking performance is not read without context.

02 · SOURCE & SCOPE

Data

The analysis uses the gated FlyRank/internship-warehouse Parquet release, described by the project as roughly 79 million daily search-performance rows. The query reads `fact_content_daily_performance` and joins `dim_content` on pseudonymous content and client hashes. The evaluated frame is not 79 million model observations: it contains 143,206 eligible content-client pairs across 45 clients after aggregation.

03 · DESIGN

Methodology

Target and feature boundary

The proxy label is 1 when April impressions are less than 80% of March impressions, and 0 otherwise. This threshold is a measurable decline screen, not a measure of content quality or the effect of an edit. Seven March-only inputs are used: impressions, clicks, average position, GA4 sessions, content age as of 31 March, the GA4 availability flag, and a position availability flag.

Client/content hashes, April impressions, the target label, trend-derived fields, and product decision outputs are excluded from model features. The leakage audit checks this feature boundary directly and confirms that primary-fold client groups do not overlap. It also checks that each predictor is measured by 31 March and before the April label window.

Models and comparator

Logistic Regression uses median imputation, standardization, and a 2,000-iteration limit. HistGradientBoosting uses median imputation, 120 iterations, at most 15 leaves, a minimum leaf size of 30, and L2 regularization of 1. The fixed Week-4 rule scores only content that is at least 180 days old, has at least 500 March impressions, and has average position of 4 or worse; its score is March impressions divided by average position plus one. Both models and the rule are evaluated on the same test rows in every fold.

Validation

The primary design is five-fold GroupKFold by client, covering 45 clients. Five 80/20 random-row splits are included as a deliberately weaker diagnostic because the same client can appear in both train and test. The seed is 42. Precision@50 and average precision are reported as unweighted means across folds with fold standard deviation; that spread is descriptive, not a confidence interval.

The model is selected by mean grouped-fold precision@50 on the same folds used to report it. This makes the result exploratory and potentially optimistic; it is not a sealed test or a time-forward test across several months.

05 · SCOPE

Limitations & honest framing

The label is a one-month thresholded impressions proxy, not human-assessed page quality, a future search outcome, or an observed response to a refresh. A page crossing the 20% threshold does not imply that editing it would help.

This is one March-to-April transition. Paired-month coverage and the March impression floor define the eligible population and may select for measurement availability. The grouped design reduces client overlap, but 45 clients and one transition do not establish broad future-month transfer.

Model selection used the same folds as evaluation. Fold standard deviations are descriptive, not confidence intervals. Scores were not calibrated; at an illustrative 0.5 threshold, the audit counted 37,681 false positives and 35,145 false negatives, so those threshold counts should not be mistaken for ranking performance. Error rates were 0.471 for content under 180 days, 0.554 for 180–365 days, and 0.525 over 365 days; these are descriptive subgroups, not validated age effects.

No refresh interventions, recovered clicks, editorial effort, revenue, or user outcomes were measured. The evidence does not support causal uplift, ROI, search-ranking prediction, or guaranteed performance. The gated source is referenced by repository path and date windows, but its content-addressed revision was not recorded; reruns may differ if source data or software versions change.

Separate target, separate study. The validation notebook also audits a click-decline analysis with a different target and population. Its results are not merged into this impressions study.
06 · ACTION PLAYBOOK

Ranked recommendations

  1. Pilot the model only as a human-reviewed ranking aid. The grouped-fold mean precision@50 is promising for this proxy, but same-fold selection makes it provisional. Decision support only
  2. Verify current measurements and eligibility before review. Check window completeness, seasonality, recent edits, and the impression floor; the measured result does not cover items outside that frame.
  3. Keep an editor between every score and every change. Review intent, current page quality, factual accuracy, compliance, and the reason for action. Record accept, defer, or reject and a short reason.
  4. Keep the rule and base rate visible in a capacity-limited pilot. Compare all three on the same held-out client groups; do not use random-row scores to justify transfer to new clients.
  5. Require a later untouched-month evaluation before scaling. Repeat over several months, assess calibration and client-level variation, and measure review cost and downstream outcomes before making uplift or ROI claims.

No-go actions: do not auto-publish, rewrite, delete, redirect, canonicalize, or assign work from this score.
